Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before joining

Is this therapy?

No. This is not therapy, counseling, or medical treatment. It is a structured, community-based social support program. It is not billable to insurance.

Do you take insurance?

No. This is a private pay, community-based program — not a clinical or medical service.

Who is this for?

Neurodivergent young adults and adults — particularly those who are autistic, AuDHD, or have social anxiety — who want connection but find typical social settings overwhelming. Participants should be able to participate in a small group setting with minimal 1:1 support.

What age range is this for?

The initial pilot is focused on young adults and adults roughly ages 18–35. We may expand age ranges in future cohorts based on interest and fit.

What if someone is shy or has difficulty talking?

That's completely okay. No one is ever pressured to talk more than they're comfortable with. Structured activities and shared tasks give people ways to connect that don't rely solely on conversation.

What if someone gets overwhelmed?

The structure is designed with that in mind. We have built-in breaks, a "step away" option, and a calm, low-stimulation environment. Participants never have to push through discomfort.

Is this for dating?

Not initially. The pilot focuses on friendship, social confidence, and genuine connection in a low-pressure setting. Future offerings may include dating-related opportunities if there is enough interest and a good fit.

Can parents or caregivers reach out?

Yes! Parents and caregivers are welcome to fill out the interest form on behalf of someone. We may follow up with the participant directly to ensure it's a good fit.

How will participants be chosen for the pilot?

The first pilot is intentionally small — 4 to 6 people. We'll review interest form responses to find people who are the best fit for the first group. Not everyone will be selected for the pilot round, but we'll keep qualified leads in mind for future cohorts.

What if someone needs more support than the group can provide?

The first pilot is designed for relatively independent participants who can engage in a small group setting. If someone's support needs are higher than the group can safely accommodate, we'll kindly let them know and help point them toward more appropriate resources if we can.

What does a typical session look like?

Each session has a clear start, a shared activity or guided interaction, and a clear end. There's no forced mingling, no awkward icebreakers, and plenty of space to participate at your own pace.

Is there a commitment?

The pilot is a small, finite experience. We'll share details about length and structure when we follow up with interested participants. Nothing is mandatory — this is designed to be low-pressure.

What makes this different from other social groups?

Small size, structured format, sensory-aware environment, optional participation, and a focus on genuine connection over performance. This was designed by someone who understands neurodivergent needs from the inside.

Still have questions? Submit the interest form and we'll follow up!
