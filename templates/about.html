Why This Exists

The story behind the program

The Need

After years of working with teens and young adults on the autism spectrum — and being a neurodivergent adult myself — I kept hearing the same things:

"I want to make friends but I don't know how."
"Typical social situations feel overwhelming."
"I feel like I have to mask all the time."
"I just want connection without the pressure."

What I Observed

Most social opportunities fall into two extremes:

🔹 Too unstructured — large groups, loud venues, forced networking. Overwhelming and hard to navigate.

🔹 Too clinical — social skills groups that feel like therapy or school. Great for some, but not what everyone wants.

What was missing was something in the middle: structured without being rigid, supportive without being clinical, and social without being overwhelming.

The Approach

This program was built from the ground up with neurodivergent needs in mind — not retrofitted into an existing framework. The structure isn't about teaching "correct" social behavior. It's about creating an environment where real connection can actually happen.

Small groups. Clear expectations. Shared activities. Low pressure. Space to be yourself.

Who I Am

I'm a local occupational therapy practitioner with prior experience supporting teenagers and young adults on the autism spectrum in a variety of settings. I've seen firsthand what happens when social opportunities don't fit neurodivergent needs — and what changes when they do.

This program is not therapy. It's a community-based social offering designed to create more accessible opportunities for genuine connection.

What This Is NOT

  • Therapy or counseling
  • Clinical services or medical care
  • Insurance-billable treatment
  • High-support care
  • A replacement for professional mental health services
