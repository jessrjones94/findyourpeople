# Session 1 — "Getting Comfortable"
## Find Your People Pilot | Facilitator Guide

**Group:** Ethan, Joe, Eric, Brittney, Brian, Jessi  
**Duration:** 2 hours  
**Goal:** Create safety, reduce uncertainty, let everyone feel seen without pressure

---

## Before the Session

**Space check (30 min before):**
- [ ] Room temperature — adjustable? Aim for cool, not cold
- [ ] Seating arranged in a loose circle (no tables blocking view of each other)
- [ ] Name tags + markers ready
- [ ] Snacks laid out (optional, low-pressure vibe)
- [ ] Bathroom location identified and pointed out
- [ ] Phone on silent
- [ ] Printed or written copy of this plan

**Materials:**
- Name tags + markers
- 3–4 conversation prompt cards (see Activity 2)
- One collaborative activity supplies (see Activity 1)
- Sign-in sheet or checklist
- Handout with next session date/time/location (optional — can be verbal)

---

## The Session

### 0:00 — Arrival (10 min)

**What to do:** Stand near the door, greet each person as they arrive. Make eye contact, say their name, offer a seat.

**What to say:**
> "Hi, I'm Jess. You must be [name] — I'm so glad you're here. Have a seat wherever feels comfortable. There's water if you need it, and feel free to use the restroom whenever — it's down the hall to the left."

**Why it matters:** First impressions set the tone. A warm, unhurried welcome signals that this space is safe.

---

### 0:10 — Welcome + Opening Circle (10 min)

**What to do:** Once everyone is seated, use a gentle verbal prompt to gather attention. Go around the circle — name and one word about how they're feeling today.

**What to say:**
> "Okay, let's get started. First — welcome, everyone. I'm really glad you're here. This first session is just about getting comfortable. Nothing you have to do perfectly.  
> 
> Before we begin — let's go around and I'll just say my name and a word for how I'm feeling today. You can do the same, or just pass. I'll start: I'm Jess, and I'm feeling... *ready*."

**Prompt for the group:**
> "Same thing — your name, and one word for how you're feeling right now. You can pass if you'd rather not."

**How to handle passing:** If someone passes, just smile and move to the next person. Don't comment on it.

**Tips:**
- Keep your tone warm, unhurried
- If the group is quiet, sit in the silence for 5–10 seconds before prompting
- Don't rush this — connection starts here

---

### 0:20 — Activity 1: Collaborative Task (25–30 min)

**Purpose:** Give people something to *do together* that doesn't require conversation. Reduces social pressure while creating shared experience.

**Option A — Collaborative Art (recommended)**
- **What:** Small group mosaic or collaborative drawing. Each person gets a section of a poster board to decorate around a theme.
- **Theme ideas:** "What does connection feel like?" / "A place that feels peaceful" / "Something that makes you smile"
- **Supplies:** Poster board, markers, colored pencils, washi tape, magazines to cut
- **Why it works:** People can contribute without speaking. Creates a shared artifact.

**Option B — Building Together**
- **What:** Simple building challenge (LEGO, toothpicks + marshmallows, etc.)
- **Supplies:** One shared kit or materials for 2–3 person mini-teams
- **Why it works:** Side-by-side activity, no eye contact required

**What to say:**
> "Okay, for the next little while we're going to do a quiet activity together. This is something we'll do as a group, but there's no right or wrong way to do it — just something to do with your hands while we're in the same space."

**During the activity:**
- Circulate gently — don't hover
- If someone looks stuck, you can offer a light prompt: "You could add whatever feels right"
- If two people start talking, let it happen naturally
- If someone works in complete silence, that's fine

---

### 0:50 — Break (10 min)

**What to do:** Announce the break, point out snacks and bathroom again, let people move freely.

**What to say:**
> "Let's take a quick break for about 10 minutes. Feel free to step outside, use the restroom, grab some water. We'll get back together in a bit."

**During break:**
- Let people drift
- You can gently approach anyone who seems isolated and say something light: "How's that treating you?"
- Don't force check-ins
- Step away yourself if you need a moment

---

### 1:00 — Activity 2: Conversation Prompts (25–30 min)

**Purpose:** Give people structured prompts so they're not staring at each other wondering what to talk about. Cards remove the burden of generating topics.

**What:** Pick from a stack of simple prompt cards. Read the card aloud, let people respond in whatever order feels natural. No going around the circle required.

**Prompt card examples — print these out:**

| Prompt |
|--------|
| What's a small thing that made you smile recently? |
| If you could have any superpower for a day, what would it be? |
| What's a TV show or movie you've enjoyed lately? |
| What's your favorite way to spend a weekend? |
| Is there a food you could eat every day for a week? |
| What's something you're looking forward to right now? |
| If you had a magic wand and could change one thing about daily life, what would it be? |
| What's a hobby you used to have that you'd like to get back into? |

**How to run it:**
1. Read the first prompt aloud
2. Wait — give people 10–15 seconds to think
3. If no one starts, you go first as a model: "I'll start — lately I've been enjoying..."
4. After 2–3 people have spoken, move to the next card

**What to say:**
> "Okay, we're going to do something a little different. I have these cards with simple questions. I'll read one out loud, and if you want to answer, you can. There's no going around the circle — just whatever feels right. You can also just listen. I'll start."

**Tips:**
- If someone rambles, it's okay — let them finish, then gently move on: "Thanks for that. Let's try another one."
- If someone says very little, don't call on them
- Keep the energy light — this isn't therapy, it's just conversation

---

### 1:30 — Closing Circle (5–10 min)

**What to do:** Gather attention back. Go around the circle — one optional thing: "One thing that felt okay about today."

**What to say:**
> "Almost time to wrap up. Before we go — I want to hear one thing from anyone who wants to share: one thing that felt okay about today. It can be really small. I'll go first: *I felt okay about how patient everyone was with each other.*  
> 
> If you'd rather pass, that's completely fine."

**After everyone (or no one) shares:**
> "Thank you all for being here today. That took courage, and I mean that. Our next session will be [day, time, location — if known]. If anything comes up before then, you can reach me at [program number] or [email]. Looking forward to seeing you again."

---

### 1:40 — Wind-Down + Departure (10 min)

**What to do:** Let people leave when they're ready. Stand near the door, make eye contact, say something warm as they leave.

**What to say (as each person leaves):**
> "I'm really glad you came. See you next time."

Or simply: "Take care, [name]."

**Don't rush anyone out.** Some people need a few extra minutes to transition. Let it happen naturally.

---

## Facilitation Notes

### What to do if someone is silent the whole time
- Don't try to draw them out
- Their silence is data — they're observing, taking it in, not ready to share
- A gentle nod or smile when making eye contact is enough

### What to do if someone dominates the conversation
- After they finish, redirect: "Thanks for that. I'd love to hear from someone else — what about the rest of you?"
- You can also gently steer: "Let's try a new question — [read next card]"

### What to do if two people seem to connect
- Let it happen
- Don't interrupt or redirect unless it's taking over the whole group

### What to do if someone seems distressed
- If they excuse themselves, you can follow up quietly: "You okay? Need anything?"
- If they want to leave early, let them go without making it a thing: "Of course, no problem. Feel free to reach out if you want to talk."
- You are not a therapist — keep the scope to facilitation, not counseling
